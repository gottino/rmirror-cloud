rMirror onboarding review

The product has a calm, coherent look and a setup wizard that is better than most at this stage. But a first-time user has to cross about twelve steps and three different interfaces before seeing their handwriting as text, and the path to Notion is broken at its three most prominent entry points.

Getting the first notebook in: hard for a non-technical user. Sign-up is easy. The difficulty sits in the handoff to the Mac agent, where the user must find a menu-bar icon, understand "Initial Sync", "Save Selection" and "page limit", and gets told "You're All Set!" before anything has arrived.

Connecting Notion: currently broken for new users on the guided path. The wizard, the onboarding checklist and the day-3 email all link to the database picker, which fails unless the user has already authorised Notion somewhere else. Users who find the Integrations page on their own get through, but face three decisions and no confirmation that anything is syncing.

Biggest lever: fix the Notion link (hours of work), make the wizard wait for a real first notebook, and reduce the agent's first run to one screen with one button.

Conversion blockers

These five stop users outright or break trust at the moment of commitment. Fix them before any visual work.

B1

"Connect Notion" leads to an error for anyone who has not connected yet

Broken

The wizard's final screen, the onboarding checklist and the "Your notes + Notion = magic" email all link to /integrations/notion/setup. That page is the second half of the flow: it lists Notion databases. Without a prior authorisation the API answers "Notion integration not found. Please complete OAuth flow first", and the dashboard shows only "Failed to list databases". The one working entry point is the "Connect Notion" button on the Integrations page, which none of the guided paths point to.

Evidence: dashboard/app/dashboard/components/SetupWizard.tsx:341, dashboard/components/OnboardingChecklist.tsx:434, backend/app/utils/email.py:849, backend/app/api/notion_oauth.py:319, dashboard/lib/api.ts:314

Fix
  • Make the setup page check connection state first. If Notion is not authorised, show a single "Connect Notion" button that starts authorisation and returns to the same page.
  • Point all three entry points at that one page so there is a single front door.
  • Replace "Failed to list databases" with the cause and the action: "Notion isn't connected yet. Connect Notion".
B2

The wizard celebrates before anything has synced

Blocker

Step 3 shows a screenshot of the agent and a "Continue" button that is always enabled. Step 4 says "You're All Set!" and offers "Go to Dashboard". That button only reloads the notebook list; while the list is empty the dashboard renders the wizard again, so the button appears to do nothing. A user who clicked through without syncing is stuck on a success screen with no notebooks and no explanation. The backend already records first_notebook_synced_at, but the wizard never reads it.

Evidence: SetupWizard.tsx:298, SetupWizard.tsx:324–337, dashboard/app/dashboard/page.tsx:932–943

Fix
  • Turn step 3 into a live waiting state, the same pattern step 2 already uses: "Waiting for your first notebook…", then "Meeting Notes · 4 of 12 pages uploaded".
  • Advance automatically when the first page arrives. Make the success screen show that page's transcribed text next to the handwriting. That is the moment the product proves itself.
  • If nothing arrives after two minutes, show targeted help: is the agent open, is a notebook selected, did you press the sync button.
B3

The first sync is the hardest step and has the least guidance

Blocker

After signing in, the user must open the agent from the menu bar and work a screen built for power users: tick notebooks, press "Save Selection", optionally set "Limit pages per notebook", then choose between "Initial Sync", "Sync Now" and "Refresh Status". Nothing is preselected. Forgetting "Save Selection" or pressing "Sync Now" instead of "Initial Sync" are easy mistakes with no feedback. The dashboard wizard can only describe this screen with a static screenshot.

Evidence: agent/app/web/templates/index.html (Notebook Selection and Actions panels), dashboard/public/agent-sync-guide.png

Fix: a first-run mode in the agent

Open the agent window automatically after sign-in, showing one screen with a sensible selection already made and one button.

Start with your 3 most recent notebooks
☑ Meeting Notes12 pages
☑ Book Club8 pages
☑ Travel Journal3 pages
Sync 3 notebooks (23 pages)
Uses 23 of your 200 pages this month · Choose different notebooks
  • Merge "Save Selection" into the sync button. Selecting and syncing is one intent.
  • Hide "Sync Now", "Refresh Status" and the page limit behind "More options" until the first sync has finished.
  • Rename "Initial Sync" to what it does ("Sync selected notebooks") and "Quota Impact" to "Pages this will use".
B4

Requirements are hidden until after sign-up

Blocker

rMirror needs a Mac and the reMarkable desktop app, already installed and synced. The landing page mentions "your Mac" once in a step description and confirms macOS-only in the fourth FAQ answer. The desktop-app requirement appears nowhere on the website; a user discovers it as a "reMarkable Folder Not Found" error inside the agent. Windows users, and anyone who signs up on a phone, create an account and then meet a single "Download for macOS" button labelled "Choose your platform".

Evidence: dashboard/app/page.tsx:753–754 (FAQ), SetupWizard.tsx:136–159, agent/app/web/templates/index.html (folder-not-found panel)

Fix
  • Put one line under the main button: "Needs a Mac with the reMarkable desktop app. Windows is on the way."
  • For Windows visitors, swap the button for "Tell me when Windows is ready" and collect the email. That is a lead rather than a churned account.
  • On a phone, replace the download step with "Email me the download link for my Mac".
  • Add a pre-flight line in wizard step 1: "Have the reMarkable desktop app open and synced" with a link to get it.
B5

The numbers contradict each other

Trust

The same user sees different allowances and prices depending on the screen:

WhereWhat it says
Landing hero and pricingBeta: 200 pages a month (30 struck through)
Wizard step 3"Your free tier includes 30 pages", shown to every user including beta
Landing pricing, Pro"Unlimited OCR pages" for $9
In-app Pro card and billing500 pages a month, "16x more!", launching "March 2026"
Landing features"Readwise highlights" with a tick; the app lists Readwise as Coming Soon

The wizard figure does the most damage: it tells a beta user to limit their first sync to 5–10 pages when they have 200, which makes the first impression smaller than it needs to be.

Evidence: dashboard/app/page.tsx:196, :492, :673; SetupWizard.tsx:291; dashboard/lib/constants.ts:17–27

Fix

Read the allowance from the quota API everywhere it is shown, wizard included. Decide what Pro is and state it once in constants.ts, then have the landing page use the same constants. Remove the past launch date and any feature that is not live.

High-friction areas

F1

Landing page: the proof is missing and the exits are prominent

High
  • No sign-in link. The page has no header at all. A returning user who lands here has no visible way in.
  • "View on GitHub" sits beside "Sign Up Free" at equal size. For a non-technical visitor it is the wrong door, and it takes them off the site.
  • The hero never shows handwriting becoming text. The lead image is a dashboard of notebook cards. The one thing a reMarkable owner wants to see, their kind of scrawl next to clean text, is five sections down and labelled "Powerful OCR Search".
  • The scroll animation moves the layout. After 250px the hero re-centres, the screenshot scales to 170% and 850px of padding is added. It delays the content and shifts the page under the reader.
  • The pain points are written as questions ("No automatic transcription to text?"), which reads as uncertain. State them.
  • Screenshots are out of date. The integrations image shows Todoist as "Coming Soon" and a Billing menu item, both no longer true, and a Next.js development badge overlaps the agent status.
  • "How It Works" starts with "Download Agent" while every button says "Sign Up". The steps and the action disagree about what comes first.

Evidence: dashboard/app/page.tsx:40–46, :76–88, :170–184, :382–399; dashboard/public/integrations-screenshot.png

Fix
  • Add a slim header: logo, Pricing, FAQ, Sign in, Sign up.
  • Make the hero a split image: a real handwritten page on the left, its transcription on the right, a Notion page below. Remove the scroll transform.
  • Demote GitHub to a text link ("Open source on GitHub") under the button.
  • Reorder to: hero with proof, three steps beginning with "Create your account", integrations, pricing, FAQ. Cut "Everything You Need"; its content repeats the rest.
F2

Consent is asked twice

Medium

The sign-up page states that creating an account means agreeing to the terms. The first thing the new user then sees is a blocking modal asking them to tick a box and agree again, before the welcome screen. It is the first interaction with the product and it is legal paperwork.

Evidence: dashboard/app/sign-up/[[...sign-up]]/page.tsx:30, dashboard/app/dashboard/page.tsx:1269, dashboard/components/TermsAcceptanceModal.tsx

Fix

Collect consent once, as a required checkbox in the sign-up form (Clerk supports a legal-consent field), and record it from the sign-up webhook. Keep the modal only for existing users when the terms change.

F3

Wizard details that cost confidence

Medium
  • The four progress dots have no labels, so the user cannot tell how much is left or what is coming. Label them: Download, Connect, Sync, Done.
  • Clicking Download moves to the next step after one second whether or not the download started (a blocked pop-up goes unnoticed). Keep the download link visible on step 2 as "Download didn't start?".
  • Steps 1 and 2 can be one screen. The install instructions are useful while the file downloads, not after another click.
  • The Gatekeeper reassurance is in small type inside step 3 of the list. It is the one moment of fear in the install; give it a small illustration of the actual dialog.
  • The project notes say the agent needs Full Disk Access to read the reMarkable folder. Neither the wizard nor the agent mentions it. Test on a clean Mac; if macOS prompts or silently blocks, this needs its own guided step.

Evidence: SetupWizard.tsx:79–88, :97–116, :198–230

F4

The agent speaks developer

Medium
  • The menu offers "Open Web UI" and "Settings", which open the same window. Use one item: "Open rMirror".
  • Settings shows Environment (Production, Staging, Custom), API URL, and an email and password form next to the browser sign-in. Move these to a hidden developer section.
  • The sign-in bridge narrates "Exchanging for agent token…". Say "Connecting your Mac…".
  • After sign-in the result page offers "Close Window" and "Return to Dashboard", where "Dashboard" means the agent, not the website the user just came from. Go straight into the first-run screen from B3.

Evidence: agent/app/tray/menu_bar.py:39–41, agent/app/web/templates/index.html (Account Settings), dashboard/app/agent/page.tsx:51, agent/app/web/templates/auth_result.html:76–83

F5

After the first sync, the dashboard stops guiding

Medium
  • The checklist's first item, "See your handwriting as text", has no button. Link it to the newest notebook that has finished transcription.
  • The sidebar always reads "Last sync: a few moments ago". The text is fixed, not measured. A user whose agent stopped a week ago is told everything is fine. Show the real time, and turn amber after a day of silence.
  • When no agent is connected the sidebar says "No Agent" with nothing to click. Make it "Set up the Mac app" and link to the wizard.
  • Page status is shown raw: "Awaiting Quota", "pending", "OCR Pending - Quota Exhausted". Use plain states: "Transcribing…", "Waiting for next month's pages (resets 1 Dec)".
  • A notebook page offers Export and Delete only. There is no sign of whether it reached Notion and no way to send it there, so the integration's value is invisible where the user spends their time.

Evidence: dashboard/components/OnboardingChecklist.tsx:415–423, dashboard/components/Sidebar.tsx:206–211, dashboard/app/notebooks/[id]/page.tsx:201, :222

Connecting Notion and other destinations

Leaving aside the broken link in B1, here is what a user who reaches the Integrations page experiences.

I1

Three decisions where one would do

High

After authorising in Notion the user waits on a two-second "Successfully Connected!" screen, then must choose between "Select Existing Database" and "Create New Database", and if creating, name it and pick an optional parent page. Most first-time users have no existing database that fits and do not know what a parent page is for.

Evidence: dashboard/app/integrations/notion/callback/page.tsx:46–49, dashboard/app/integrations/notion/setup/page.tsx:189–321

Fix

After authorisation, create "rMirror Notebooks" automatically and start syncing. Show one confirmation screen with the result and a quiet link, "Use a database I already have", for the few who need it.

I2

No confirmation and no way to see the result

High

Choosing a database redirects to /integrations?success=true, but the Integrations page does not read that parameter, so nothing acknowledges the setup. The backend starts an initial sync of existing notebooks in the background and exposes its status, yet the screen shows only "Status: Connected". There is no link to the Notion database. The user has to open Notion and search to find out whether it worked.

Evidence: notion/setup/page.tsx:93, :121; dashboard/app/integrations/page.tsx:332–339; backend/app/api/notion_oauth.py (initial sync status endpoint)

Fix

End the flow on a result: "Sending 3 notebooks to Notion… 14 of 23 pages" with a prominent "Open in Notion" button. Keep that link permanently on the Notion row.

I3

The page is organised by data type, not by destination

Medium
  • Notion appears twice, as "Notion" under Notebooks and "Notion Todos" under Todos, each with its own setup. Show one Notion row with two switches: Notebooks and To-dos.
  • Obsidian's connect button produces an API key and the instruction to paste it into "the rMirror Obsidian plugin", with no link to the plugin and no install steps. Add both, numbered.
  • Services are shown as coloured letter tiles (N, O, T, R). Real logos are recognised faster and signal that the integration is real.
  • "Test Connection" is a debugging tool. Replace it with an automatic health state: "Working · last sent 2 hours ago" or "Needs reconnecting".
  • Disconnecting uses the browser's native confirm box, which looks unlike the rest of the product. Use the modal style already built for notebook deletion.
  • Integrations sits at the bottom of the sidebar under a spacer. For a product whose promise is "your notes in your tools", it deserves the second position.

Evidence: dashboard/app/integrations/page.tsx:119, :322–634; dashboard/components/Sidebar.tsx:139–152

UI style

The warm paper palette suits the subject and is applied consistently across dashboard, agent and email. It reads as a considered product, not a template. The weaknesses are legibility, hierarchy and a few inconsistencies.

S1

Secondary text and buttons fall below contrast guidelines

High

The instructions a new user most needs to read are set in the faintest colour. Darken warm gray to about #6b6661 and terracotta to about #b24a44; the character of the palette is unchanged. This report uses those two values.

Evidence: dashboard/app/globals.css:12–17

S2

One colour does three jobs

Medium

Terracotta marks the current page in the sidebar, the selected view toggle and the primary action. On the dashboard that puts two or three solid terracotta blocks on screen, none of which is the thing to do next. Keep solid terracotta for the single main action on a screen. Show selection with a tinted background and dark text. The black "Connect Notion" button is a fourth style; give all connect buttons the standard primary look with the service logo inside.

S3

Notebook cards are mostly empty

Medium

In grid view each card is a tall rectangle with three lines of text at the top and blank space below. Show the actual first-page thumbnail (a PDF is already generated for every page) with the title beneath. Handwriting thumbnails make notebooks recognisable at a glance and make the dashboard feel like the user's own notes.

S4

Styling by hand produces small inconsistencies

Medium

Most styling is inline, with hover states set through mouse-event handlers (86 inline style blocks on the dashboard page alone). The consequences are visible to users: hover effects that do not respond to keyboard focus, no visible focus ring on most buttons, and sizes that drift between screens. A small set of shared Button, Card and StatusBadge components would fix this and make the changes in this report cheaper to ship.

Evidence: dashboard/app/dashboard/page.tsx, dashboard/components/OnboardingChecklist.tsx:78–121

What already works

Keep these; several are the pattern to extend elsewhere.

  • The wizard detects the agent by itself. Step 2 polls every five seconds and advances with a short confirmation. Step 3 should behave the same way.
  • The wizard resumes where the user left off after a refresh, using server-side progress.
  • Agent sign-in happens in the browser with a "Continue as…" confirmation, so there is no password to type into an unfamiliar app.
  • Running out of pages does not block uploads. Notebooks still arrive and are transcribed when the allowance resets. That is generous and well explained in the FAQ.
  • The agent's "folder not found" screen tells the user exactly what to install and do.
  • Lifecycle emails follow real progress (set-up reminder, first sync, first transcription, Notion, feedback) instead of a fixed calendar.
  • "Start small" is the right advice for a metered product. It only needs the correct number.

Proposed flow

The aim is one continuous path with the product doing the detecting and choosing, and with each step ending on visible proof.

Today · about 12 steps, 3 interfaces
  1. Sign up
  2. Accept terms in a modal
  3. Download
  4. Read install steps on a second screen
  5. Install, launch, sign in
  6. Find the menu-bar icon, open the window
  7. Tick notebooks, Save Selection
  8. Set a page limit, press Initial Sync
  9. Click Continue, then Go to Dashboard
  10. Wait, refresh, open a notebook
  11. Find Integrations, connect Notion
  12. Choose or create a database, then look in Notion
Proposed · 6 steps, each ends on proof
  1. Sign up with consent as a checkbox in the form. Requirements stated beside the button.
  2. Download and install on one screen, instructions visible while the file downloads. The page waits and confirms "Mac connected".
  3. One button in the agent: "Sync 3 notebooks (23 pages)", opened automatically after sign-in.
  4. Watch it arrive. The website shows pages uploading, then opens the first transcribed page beside the handwriting.
  5. "Send this to Notion" offered on that page. One authorisation; the database is created automatically.
  6. "Open in Notion" shows the same note there. Setup is complete; mention To-dos, Obsidian and Todoist as next options.

Placing the Notion prompt on the first transcribed page matters more than its wording. At that moment the user has just seen the product work and the next question in their head is "can I get this into my tools?".

How to tell whether it worked

The product already records most of the events needed (account_created, agent_downloaded, onboarding_step, integration_setup_started, integration_connected, notion_database_configured). Build one funnel in Umami from them and add the two that are missing: agent connected and first notebook received. Track three numbers before and after: the share of sign-ups who get a first notebook within 24 hours, the median time from sign-up to first transcribed page, and the share of users with a notebook who connect a destination within seven days.

Method and limits

This is an expert review of the code and shipped assets on the main branch: the landing page, sign-up, setup wizard, dashboard, notebook view, integrations and Notion setup, the Mac agent's menu and local window, the sign-in bridge, and the onboarding emails. Screens were assessed from their source and from the screenshots in dashboard/public.

  • I did not run the live product or install the agent on a Mac, so timing, macOS permission prompts and the real Notion authorisation screen were not observed. The Full Disk Access point in F3 needs a clean-machine test.
  • No analytics data was available. Severity ratings are judgement based on the flow, not measured drop-off. The funnel above will show which step loses the most people.
  • No user sessions were observed. Five moderated first-run sessions with reMarkable owners who have not seen the product would confirm or reorder these priorities quickly.
  • Contrast ratios in S1 are calculated from the colour values in globals.css.
